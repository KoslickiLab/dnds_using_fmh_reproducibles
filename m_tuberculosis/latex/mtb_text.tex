\subsection*{A collection of closely related genomes: \textit{Mycobacterium tuberculosis}}

The biological applications above compare genomes of different species or genera. The opposite case,
thousands of isolates of one species, is where an alignment-free estimator should pay off most,
because the number of pairs grows as the square of the collection while the cost of each pair does
not fall. \citet{gan2025} provide a direct test. In a collection of \mtbRunsGan{}
\textit{M.\ tuberculosis} isolates they found an ordering of the groups of genes: per-gene ratios of
non-synonymous to synonymous mutations ($p_N/p_S$, counted from mutation events on phylogenies of
the isolates) of 0.69 for essential genes, 0.84 for non-essential genes and 0.90 for the PE/PPE gene
family. They also compared pairwise \dnds between groups of genes, with the \texttt{kaks} function of
the R package \texttt{seqinr} on concatenated gene-group sequences, but restricted that pairwise
analysis to a random 5,000 isolates because of its computational cost. We asked whether pairwise
\method, computed over every pair of the collection, recovers the same ordering.

\subsubsection*{Isolates and sequences}

Of the \mtbRunsGan{} sequencing runs listed by \citet{gan2025}, \mtbWithAssembly{} have an assembly
in AllTheBacteria release 0.2 \citep{hunt2024}, of which we used \mtbAssemblyIsolates{}; for a further
\mtbReadIsolates{} runs without an assembly we called substitutions from the reads. As in
\citet{gan2025}, each isolate is represented by the inferred ancestral sequence of the
\textit{M.\ tuberculosis} complex (MTBC), the group of closely related lineages that cause
tuberculosis \citep{comas2010,comas2013}, with that isolate's substitutions applied; insertions and
deletions are not applied. The ancestral sequence has the length and coordinates of H37Rv, the
laboratory reference strain of \textit{M.\ tuberculosis} \citep{cole1998}, so the coding sequences
of the H37Rv annotation can be cut from it and translated. Isolates carry \mtbMeanSubstitutions{}
substitutions on average. Comparing isolates through these sequences, rather than through the gene
calls of their own draft assemblies, is necessary at this divergence, for the reason given at the end
of this section. Genes were assigned to groups from published tables only: essentiality from
\citet{dejesus2017} (\mtbGenesEssential{} essential and \mtbGenesNonessential{} non-essential genes),
PE/PPE membership from the H37Rv annotation (\mtbGenesPeppe{} genes), and the high-quality segments
of those genes from \citet{gan2025}, which is the sequence over which they computed their PE/PPE value
(\mtbBasesPeppehq{} of \mtbBasesPeppe{} bases).

\subsubsection*{Every pair of the collection}

All values were computed with our implementation of \method, with the parameters used throughout
this work (\kaa{}$= 7$, so $k_{nt} = 21$; max containment; the constant 0.77/2.23), and with every
\kmer, that is a scale factor of 1, unless stated otherwise. For a collection like this one, many
genomes that are all close to one reference, the implementation has a mode
(\texttt{--mode reference}) that computes the containments of all pairs from the same sketches,
without comparing every pair of sketches in full. Because the sketch $S_i$ of every isolate differs
from the sketch $R$ of the reference by only a few thousand hashes, the mode keeps for each isolate
the reference hashes it lacks, $L_i = R \setminus S_i$, and the hashes it adds,
$A_i = S_i \setminus R$, and uses
\[
|S_i \cap S_j| = |R| - |L_i| - |L_j| + |L_i \cap L_j| + |A_i \cap A_j| ,
\]
where the last two terms of all pairs come from one sparse matrix product. The identity is exact, so
the containments, and the \dnds values computed from them, are those of a direct pairwise comparison.
For all \mtbFullPairs{} pairs of the \mtbAllIsolates{} isolates, sketching took \mtbSketchMinutes{}
minutes, reading the sketches \mtbFullLoadMinutes{} minutes and computing the pairs
\mtbFullPairsMinutes{} minutes, on \mtbCores{} cores. \texttt{seqinr}'s \texttt{kaks}, on the same
sequences, computes \mtbSeqinrPairsPerSecond{} pairs of whole coding genomes per second on one core,
which is \mtbSeqinrFiveThousandCoreDays{} core-days for every pair of the 5,000 isolates
\citet{gan2025} used and \mtbSeqinrAllCoreYears{} core-years for the whole collection.

Including the read-derived isolates moves the median whole-genome value only from
\mtbMedianWhole{} to \mtbFullMedian{}. The analyses below nevertheless use only the
\mtbAssemblyIsolates{} assembly-derived isolates (\mtbPairs{} pairs), so that the substitutions of
every isolate were found from the same kind of data.

\subsubsection*{The whole coding genome}

Over the whole coding genome the median is \mtbMedianWhole{} (quartiles \mtbQuartilesWhole{}), and
\mtbBelowOne\% of pairs fall below 1, as expected when purifying selection dominates. The value rises
modestly with the distance between the two isolates of a pair: \mtbMedianSameSublineage{} for pairs
within a sublineage, \mtbMedianSameLineage{} for pairs from different sublineages of one lineage and
\mtbMedianDifferentLineage{} for pairs from different lineages (Figure~\ref{fig:mtb_main}a and d).
The rare most divergent pairs, the \mtbTailShare\% that differ at more than \mtbTailThreshold\% of
sites, reach higher values. Of these, \mtbTailWithOutlier\% involve one of \mtbTailIsolates{}
isolates that carry a median of \mtbTailSubstitutions{} substitutions, more than twice the usual
number; substitutions that are errors rather than mutations fall on synonymous and non-synonymous
sites in proportion to their number and pull the estimate towards 1.

\subsubsection*{The ordering of the gene groups}

The ordering \citet{gan2025} report is recovered (Figure~\ref{fig:mtb_main}b). Essential genes give
the lowest value (median \mtbMedianEssential{}), the whole coding genome lies above them
(\mtbMedianWhole{}), non-essential genes higher still (\mtbMedianNonessential{}), and the
high-quality segments of the PE/PPE genes are the highest (\mtbMedianPeppehq{}). The absolute values
are not comparable with the $p_N/p_S$ values of \citet{gan2025}, which come from a different
estimator; the ordering is. It holds pair by pair, not only between
medians: on a subsample of \mtbPairedPairsMillion{} million pairs shared by all groups, non-essential
genes exceed essential genes in \mtbShareNonessentialOverEssential\% of pairs, and the PE/PPE segments
exceed non-essential genes in \mtbSharePeppehqOverNonessential\% (Wilcoxon signed-rank test on
\mtbWilcoxonPairs{} of those pairs, $p < 10^{\mtbWilcoxonPExponent}$ in both cases). Taken over whole genes instead of the
high-quality segments, the PE/PPE family is not elevated (\mtbMedianPeppe{}; above the non-essential
genes in only \mtbSharePeppeOverNonessential\% of pairs). The repetitive remainder of those genes is
where short-read data is least reliable, which is why \citet{gan2025} restricted their analysis to
the segments; the conventional estimator gives the same answer on these sequences (below), so this is
a property of the sequences rather than of the estimator.

\subsubsection*{Agreement with the conventional estimate}

To compare \method with the pairwise estimate \citet{gan2025} used, we ran \texttt{seqinr}'s \texttt{kaks} on
the same sequences for \mtbSeqinrIsolates{} isolates chosen at random (\mtbSeqinrPairs{} pairs; the
cost of \texttt{seqinr} rules out many more), and took the \method values of the same pairs from the
run above. The two estimates rank the pairs alike: the rank correlation between them is
\mtbSpearmanSeqinrEssential{} for essential genes, \mtbSpearmanSeqinrNonessential{} for non-essential
genes and \mtbSpearmanSeqinrWhole{} for the whole coding genome (Figure~\ref{fig:mtb_main}c). They
order four of the groups alike: essential genes, the whole coding genome, non-essential genes and the
PE/PPE segments, from lowest to highest. Whole PE/PPE genes are placed differently, below every other
group by \texttt{seqinr} (median \mtbSeqinrMedianPeppe{}) and between the whole coding genome and the
non-essential genes by \method (\mtbToolMedianPeppe{}); neither places them above the non-essential
genes. For the other four groups \method returns lower values than \texttt{seqinr}
(whole coding genome: \mtbToolMedianWhole{} against \mtbSeqinrMedianWhole{}). The ratio of synonymous to
non-synonymous sites in these sequences, \mtbSiteRatioWhole{}, is close to the \mtbConstant{} the
estimator assumes, so this offset does not come from that constant.

\subsubsection*{Where sketching breaks down}

Everything above uses every \kmer. Each value rests on the hashes that differ between the two
isolates of a pair: a median of \mtbDifferingProteinWhole{} protein hashes per pair for the whole
coding genome, \mtbDifferingProteinEssential{} for the essential genes and
\mtbDifferingProteinPeppehq{} for the PE/PPE segments. A sketch with scale factor $s$ keeps about
$1/s$ of them, and the values from a sketch follow those from every \kmer less and less closely as $s$
grows (Figure~\ref{fig:mtb_scale}b). For the whole coding genome, the rank correlation between the
values from a sketch and those from every \kmer, over the same pairs, is \mtbSpearmanWholeScaleTwo{},
\mtbSpearmanWholeScaleFive{} and \mtbSpearmanWholeScaleTen{} at scale factors 2, 5 and 10; for the
PE/PPE segments, the group with the fewest differing hashes, it is \mtbSpearmanPeppehqScaleTwo{},
\mtbSpearmanPeppehqScaleFive{} and \mtbSpearmanPeppehqScaleTen{}. The medians drift downwards as well
(whole coding genome: \mtbMedianWhole{}, \mtbMedianWholeScaleFive{} and \mtbMedianWholeScaleTen{} at
1, 5 and 10; Figure~\ref{fig:mtb_scale}c). The comparisons between groups last longer than the
individual values (Figure~\ref{fig:mtb_scale}d). Essential genes stay below non-essential genes in
\mtbHoldsEssentialBelowNonessentialScaleTen\% of pairs at a scale factor of 10. The elevation of the
PE/PPE segments over non-essential genes weakens steadily, from
\mtbHoldsNonessentialBelowPeppehqScaleOne\% of pairs with every \kmer to
\mtbHoldsNonessentialBelowPeppehqScaleFive\% at 5 and \mtbHoldsNonessentialBelowPeppehqScaleTen\% at
10, and is reversed at 100 (\mtbHoldsNonessentialBelowPeppehqScaleHundred\%), where a pair has a
median of \mtbDifferingProteinPeppehqScaleHundred{} differing protein hash left in that group. There
is no sharp threshold: for isolates of one species, which differ at about one site in several
thousand, a sketch has to keep every \kmer, or nearly every one, and the scale factors used between
genera in this work leave too few differing hashes to estimate anything. In this setting the saving
comes from comparing each genome with a reference once, not from sketching.

\subsubsection*{Comparing draft assemblies directly}

Comparing two independently assembled draft genomes through their own gene calls, with every \kmer,
gives a median of \mtbAssemblyMedianDnds{} over \mtbSmokePairs{} pairs of \mtbSmokeIsolates{}
isolates, and \mtbAssemblyUnusable\% of pairs give no usable value. The nucleotide and protein
containments then come out nearly equal ($1 - C_{nt} = \mtbAssemblyCnt$ against
$1 - C_{aa} = \mtbAssemblyCaa$; Figure~\ref{fig:mtb_scale}a), because two draft assemblies differ in
gene content (broken contigs, fragmented genes) as much as in sequence, and a missing gene removes
nucleotide and protein \kmers in the same proportion. The denominator of the estimate depends on the
gap between the two containments, so it collapses. This is the same failure as in the genome pairs of
Figure~\ref{fig:hier_edge}, where most of the difference between two genomes was not substitutions.

\subsubsection*{Limitations}

The sequences compared are the reference with each isolate's substitutions applied, so insertions,
deletions and genes absent from the reference are not represented, and positions an assembly does not
cover carry the reference base; the hashes in the identity above are those of these sequences. For the
\mtbReadsAssemblyIsolates{} isolates that have both an assembly and reads, substitutions found the two
ways give rank-correlated values (\mtbReadsAssemblySpearman{}; median difference
$\mtbReadsAssemblyShift$) but not identical ones. The absolute values depend on the estimator and its
constant, and should be compared with each other rather than with values from a different method.
